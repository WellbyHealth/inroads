Some weeks the referral team meeting feels like a status report nobody asked for. Other weeks it runs long because someone found a problem and the whole hour went to it. A 30-minute structure that repeats each week fixes both. People know what to prepare, and the real issues still get room.

## Why hold a weekly referral team meeting at all?

Because referrals move fast and people forget. A discharge planner mentions a new unit on Tuesday. An intake coordinator hears a complaint on Wednesday. By Friday, no one remembers who said what. A short meeting turns scattered observations into shared knowledge and clear next steps.

It also keeps marketing, liaison and intake looking at the same facts. When those groups work from different lists, they argue about whose numbers are right. When they look at one page together, they argue about what to do, which is a much better argument.

## Who should come?

The people who touch referrals: liaisons or business development, the intake lead, and whoever manages the team. A clinical leader can join occasionally, or send questions in advance. Keep it small. A meeting with ten people turns into a presentation.

## What does a 30-minute agenda look like?

Here's a pattern that tends to work. Adjust the minutes to your team.

1. Last week's numbers (5 minutes). Referrals received, admitted, declined and still pending. Just the facts, from the page you printed or shared.
2. Stuck referrals (5 minutes). Anything waiting too long, and who will unstick it today.
3. Sources to watch (7 minutes). A source that's up, one that's down, and any that went quiet. One sentence of "why do we think so?" each.
4. Visits and calls this week (5 minutes). Who's visiting whom, and what we want to learn or ask.
5. Barriers and feedback (5 minutes). Anything partners told us, or anything slowing our own response.
6. Owners and dates (3 minutes). Read the action list back aloud.

If something needs a longer conversation, put it on a separate call with only the people involved. The weekly meeting is for noticing, not solving everything.

## What numbers should you bring?

One page, the same layout each week. Resist the urge to add more. A good page shows:

- Referrals received this week and this month so far.
- The same figure for the same period last month, for a rough comparison.
- Pending referrals with days waiting.
- The top five sources and their counts.
- Sources that sent steadily before and have sent nothing lately.

Keep the arithmetic simple so people trust it.

> **Illustrative example**
>
> A small home health team reviews one week's page.
>
> - Referrals received: 18
> - Admitted: 11
> - Declined by the agency: 3
> - Pending: 4
>
> That adds up to 18 (11 + 3 + 4). Admissions are 11 of 18, about 61%. The 4 pending are the first thing to discuss, especially if any have waited more than a couple of days. The team spends the first ten minutes on them and the rest of the meeting on sources.

## How do you keep the meeting from becoming a gripe session?

Set two ground rules. First, every problem raised comes with either a proposed fix or a person who will find one. Second, no side conversations about individual patients beyond what the work requires. Keep patient details out of the discussion where you can, and follow your own privacy policies and your counsel's guidance on what's appropriate to share in a team setting.

Celebrate small wins too. A planner who sent a thank-you note, a quiet source that sent a referral, a faster response time. A meeting that is only problems is a meeting people dread.

## How do you make sure things get done?

Write the action list during the meeting, not afterward. Each item has a person and a date. Read it back at the end. At the next meeting, start by checking last week's list. If items repeatedly slip, that's information: either the task was too big, or the owner has too much on their plate.

A shared document or a simple board is enough. The tool matters less than doing it every week.

## What if you only have 15 minutes?

Cut to three things: pending referrals, sources to watch, and owners and dates. Skip the rest and fold it into a message afterward.

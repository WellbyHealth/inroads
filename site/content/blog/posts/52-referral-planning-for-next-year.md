Every year someone says, "We need a plan." What often follows is a long document that nobody opens after February. Referral planning for next year works better when it's small, honest and tied to real relationships. It can be done in a couple of focused sessions, without drama.

## Where should referral planning for next year start?

With a look back. Before you decide where to go, spend an hour on where you've been. Pull referrals by source for the past four quarters. Note which grew, which shrank, which went quiet and which are new. Add what you remember: who retired, which hospital changed its discharge process, what your own capacity looked like.

Write down three things that surprised you. They're often the most valuable part of the exercise, because they point to relationships you assumed were stable and weren't.

## What should you decide before you pick targets?

A few honest questions about your own organization. Can you take more referrals, in the services and places you want? Is your staffing ready? Is your response time good enough that a stronger outreach effort won't just bring in referrals you can't handle?

Growth that outruns capacity damages the relationships you're building. Sometimes the right plan is to improve conversion on the referrals you already receive, which is often faster than finding new sources.

## How many sources should you focus on?

Fewer than you think. For a small team, five to eight focus sources can fill a year. Split them roughly into three kinds: sources to protect, sources to grow and sources to win back.

For each one, write one sentence on why it's on the list, one action, one owner and one date. If you can't write the "why," it's probably not a focus source.

> **Illustrative example**
>
> A small hospice team has two liaisons and 6 hours a week each for visits.
>
> - Weekly visit time: 12 hours (2 liaisons x 6 hours).
> - Planned focus sources: 6.
> - Time per source per week: 2 hours.
>
> With 12 hours across 6 sources, each gets 2 hours a week. If the team added four more focus sources, 10 in all, each would get 1.2 hours. The team decides to keep the list at 6 and use any spare time on the quiet sources.

## What actions belong in the plan?

Plain, specific, doable ones. Visit the case management lead at the hospital that dropped. Send a thank-you to the clinic that started referring. Write a one-page summary of services and update it each quarter. Fix the weekend response gap. Review the source naming list.

Mix relationship actions with process actions. The best outreach in the world doesn't help if referrals sit in a queue.

Keep every action within your rules. Don't plan anything that offers value for referrals or steers patients, and patient choice stands. Rules differ by program and state, so run any new event or program past your counsel.

## How do you handle partners who may change?

Assume some will. Hospitals reorganize, physicians retire, planners take new jobs. Build a short list of "people to re-check" each quarter, and keep notes on who your contacts are at each focus source, with a backup name where possible. A relationship that rests on one person is fragile.

Also leave unassigned time in the calendar. A new clinic opens, a partner calls with a problem, a discharge planner moves and needs a fresh introduction. A plan that fills every hour has no room for any of this.

## How will you know if the plan is working?

Pick a small number of signals now, not later. Maybe it's referrals from focus sources, the first-response time, and the number of quiet sources that came back. Write down where you are today so next year you have a baseline.

Schedule a check at mid-year, and another at the end of each quarter. Be willing to drop an action that isn't working and replace it. A plan that never changes is probably being ignored.

## What should the final document look like?

Two pages. One with the look back: three surprises and the numbers behind them. One with the plan: focus sources, actions, owners, dates and the signals you'll watch. Share it with the intake team and leadership so everyone knows what's coming.

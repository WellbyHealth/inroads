A weekly plan for home health and hospice marketers

By the Wellby team · November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Spend the week in three buckets: protect top partners, win back quiet ones, open new ones.
  • Plan Monday, visit Tuesday to Thursday, follow up and review on Friday.
  • Log outcomes, not just activity, so next week’s plan gets smarter.

Referral marketing rewards consistency more than heroics. The liaisons who grow a territory tend to run the same simple week, over and over, with a clear idea of who they're seeing and why. Here's a template to adapt.

Split the week into three buckets

Every visit should belong to one of three groups:

  • Protect: your top partners. Regular, useful touches so nobody else becomes their default.
  • Win back: partners whose referrals have slowed relative to their normal rhythm.
  • Open: high-potential sources who have never referred to you, or rarely do.

How you divide the week depends on your territory: a new agency will spend more time opening doors, while a mature one will spend more protecting what it has.

Monday: plan

Spend an hour building the week. Look at what changed last week: new referrals, partners going quiet, replies waiting on you. Pick the specific people you'll see in each bucket, group them by geography to cut drive time, and book what you can in advance. Discharge planners and practice managers have busy calendars, so a booked fifteen minutes beats a drop-in.

Tuesday to Thursday: visits

Bring a reason for every visit. A visit with something useful in hand gets remembered: an update on a shared patient's progress (shared appropriately and without unnecessary detail), a new service, an answer to a question they asked last time, or a quick in-service for their staff. Lunches and treats open doors, but on their own they rarely change where patients go.

Before you leave, agree on a next step, even a small one: "I'll send you our weekend intake number," or "Can I come back after your new case manager starts?"

Friday: follow up and review

Send every promised follow-up the same day if you can, and certainly before the weekend. Thank-you notes for new referrals go out too. Then spend twenty minutes reviewing: which visits moved something, which partners need another touch next week, and which sources to drop from the open list because they're a poor fit.

Log outcomes, not just activity

"Visited Mercy General" isn't very useful six months later. "Met the new discharge lead; she didn't know we take weekend admissions; sent our intake line" is. Capture who you met, what they needed and what you agreed. Then, over the following weeks, watch whether referrals from that source change. That's how next Monday's plan gets better than this one.

Keep it light

If logging takes longer than the visit, people stop doing it. A plan that fits on one page and a log that takes a minute per visit will beat a perfect system nobody uses.

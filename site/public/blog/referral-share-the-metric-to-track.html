Referral share: the one number post-acute marketers should track

By the Wellby team · November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Referral share is the portion of a source’s outgoing patients that come to you.
  • It separates "this partner sends fewer patients" from "this partner sends fewer patients to us".
  • Judge outreach by whether it moves, not by how many visits were logged.

Most referral teams report activity: visits made, lunches delivered, calls logged. Activity is easy to count, but it doesn't tell you whether any of it is working. A marketer can make forty visits in a month and win nothing, or make ten and double a key relationship.

The number that connects effort to results is referral share.

What referral share means

For any source, referral share is the portion of the patients they refer out, for the services you provide, that come to you. Put simply: of all the patients a partner sends out for care like yours, how many come to you?

Raw referral counts can't separate two very different situations:

  • The hospital is sending fewer patients to everyone, because of seasonality, a unit closure or a census dip.
  • The hospital is sending the same number of patients but fewer to you, because a competitor got there first.

Referral share tells you which one is happening. The first is a market condition. The second is a problem your team can fix.

Why it's hard to see

Your own referral counts only tell half the story. The other half, how many patients a partner sends to everyone, isn't in your records, and the market data that's available is often incomplete or out of date, especially for private-pay care. That's why most teams fall back to counting activity instead.

Even a rough, consistent view is far better than none. What matters is that you look at it the same way every period, so that changes mean something.

How to use it

Find the upside. A busy source where you get only a small slice is often a bigger opportunity than a smaller partner you already serve well.

Spot problems early. If your share of a partner slips while their overall volume holds steady, someone else is winning that relationship. That's the moment for a visit, not six months later.

Judge outreach by outcome. When a marketer works a source, look at whether share moved in the following months. Over time you'll learn which kinds of outreach (an in-service, a lunch, a quick update on a shared patient) actually change behavior with which kinds of partners.

Pair it with two other numbers

Share on its own can mislead, so read it alongside:

  • Referral-to-admission rate: how many referrals from a source become clients. A source can send a lot and convert poorly if the referrals don't fit your services or your payer mix.
  • Two-way balance: for facilities, how many patients you send them versus how many they send you. Partnerships that only run one direction tend not to last.

Make it a habit

Look at the trend, not a single number, and review it with the team on a regular rhythm. The point isn't a perfect dashboard. It's a regular conversation about where to spend the team's time.
